# 6.3. Activation-Level Tool Monitoring — Sparse Probes and Gated Steering

> **Source:** Ke, Yin, Zhao & Huang, *A Few Neurons Reveal When LLMs Misuse Tools:
> Sparse Detection and Selective Steering for Reliable Tool Use*
> (arXiv:2608.00218, Aug 2026).
> Companion to
> [6.2. Production Trajectory Monitoring — Sampling, Staging, and the Long-Context Cliff](./6.2.%20Production%20Trajectory%20Monitoring%20—%20Sampling%2C%20Staging%2C%20and%20the%20Long-Context%20Cliff.md),
> which monitors the trajectory from outside the model. This note covers the
> opposite placement: monitoring from inside it. For the deterministic side of
> tool safety see
> [Tool-Call Reliability](../5.%20AI%20Agents%20%26%20Tool%20Use/Tool-Call%20Reliability%20—%20Idempotency%2C%20Postcondition%20Verification%2C%20and%20Replay-Safe%20Authorization.md);
> for the interface side see
> [11.18. Tool Architecture — The Interface as a Behavioral Knob](../11.%20Harness%20Engineering/11.18.%20Tool%20Architecture%20—%20The%20Interface%20as%20a%20Behavioral%20Knob.md).

Every production guardrail on tool calling sits *outside* the model: a schema
validator, an LLM judge on the tool-call span, a policy engine on the argument
payload. All of them share two costs — they run after the model has already
committed to a call, and they cost another inference pass. This paper reports that
the three tool-use failures engineers actually care about are linearly readable off
a handful of MLP neurons, two of them *before the first token of the call is
generated*. If that holds, the guardrail moves inside the model and gets roughly
three orders of magnitude cheaper.

The claim to treat carefully is not "probes work" — probes work for many
behaviours. It is the sparsity and the placement.

---

## 1. The three failures, named properly

The paper's taxonomy is worth adopting independently of its method, because most
teams collapse all of this into "the agent called the wrong tool".

| Failure | Definition | Where it hurts |
|---|---|---|
| **Validity** | The call is made, but arguments are malformed or wrong | Retries, corrupt writes, silent no-ops |
| **Over-calling** | A tool is invoked when the answer needed no tool | Latency, token burn, unnecessary blast radius |
| **Missing** | No tool is called when one was required | Hallucinated answers presented as retrieved fact |

These are distinct failure modes with distinct fixes, and they trade off against
each other. Any prompt-level intervention that suppresses over-calling ("only use
tools when necessary") pushes the model toward *missing*, and vice versa. That
tradeoff is the reason unconditional steering is a bad idea and why the paper's
gating matters.

> **Why it matters:** validity is a *post-hoc* property — you need the generated
> arguments to judge it. Over-calling and missing are *pre-hoc* — they are decided
> before generation starts. That split determines where a monitor can physically sit.

---

## 2. Where the signal lives

PRISMS (Probing Representations In Support of Monitoring and Steering) selects
contribution-critical MLP neurons and fits an L1-regularised detector on their
activations. Two read points, matching the pre-/post-hoc split above:

```text
   prompt + tool schemas
        │
        ├──► read point A: last prompt-boundary position
        │        detects: over-calling, missing        ROC-AUC 0.90–1.00
        │        cost: one forward pass already being paid for
        ▼
   generation of the tool-call span
        │
        └──► read point B: over the generated call
                 detects: validity                    ROC-AUC 0.86–0.90
                 cost: still zero extra inference
```

The reported readout widths are the interesting number:

| Failure | Neurons needed | vs. dense residual-stream baseline |
|---|---|---|
| Missing | 1–2 | matched or beaten |
| Over-calling | 2–16 | matched or beaten |
| Validity | ~128 | matched or beaten |

Sparse detectors matched or outperformed dense residual-stream baselines using
23–627× fewer features, across six models from the Qwen3, Llama, and Gemma
families. Cross-family replication is what separates this from a single-model
curiosity — it suggests the representation is a property of instruction-tuned
tool-calling models generally, not of one checkpoint.

> **Architectural takeaway:** a one-to-two-neuron readout is not a model. It is a
> dot product on a slice of an activation tensor you already computed. The
> monitoring cost collapses from "another LLM call" to "arithmetic in the serving
> loop".

---

## 3. Detection and intervention on one basis

The part that makes this a *closed loop* rather than a probe paper: the same
failure-specific neuron basis used for detection also supports activation steering
in both directions — suppressing unnecessary calls and eliciting omitted ones.

So the monitor and the actuator are the same object. Detection score crosses
threshold → steer along the basis that produced the score. Reported result across
all six models: pooled over-calling rate down 80% (0.131 → 0.026) while
tool-required accuracy went *up* 14.2 points (0.689 → 0.831).

That both numbers moved the right way is the finding. The naive version of this
intervention moves them in opposite directions, which is exactly why the gate
exists: PRISMS conditions intervention on predicted failure risk instead of
steering unconditionally, precisely to avoid the collateral damage that
always-on steering causes.

| Design | Over-calling | Tool-required accuracy |
|---|---|---|
| No intervention | baseline 0.131 | baseline 0.689 |
| Unconditional steering | improves | pays for it elsewhere (the tradeoff in §1) |
| **Risk-gated steering** | **0.026** | **0.831** |

> **Lesson:** the gate is not an optimisation. It is what makes steering safe to
> ship. An intervention that fires on every turn is a prompt change with extra
> steps, and it inherits the same over-/under-calling seesaw.

## 4. What this changes about the monitoring funnel

[6.2](./6.2.%20Production%20Trajectory%20Monitoring%20—%20Sampling%2C%20Staging%2C%20and%20the%20Long-Context%20Cliff.md)
describes a funnel whose entire design pressure comes from monitors being
expensive: you subsample, you stage cheap-then-expensive, you summarise
hierarchically to hit a fixed human budget. An activation probe changes the
economics at the top of that funnel, and only there.

| Layer | Trajectory monitor (6.2) | Activation probe (this note) |
|---|---|---|
| Placement | Outside the model, on logged traces | Inside the serving loop |
| Timing | After the fact | Before the call executes |
| Marginal cost | Another inference pass | Effectively zero |
| Sampling | Must subsample | Can run on 100% of turns |
| Scope | Any behaviour describable in prose | Only behaviours with a trained basis |
| Access required | Logs | **Weights and activations** |
| Interpretability | Reads a rationale | Reads a scalar |

The last two rows are the constraints that decide whether you can use this at all.

**You need white-box access.** This is unavailable behind a hosted API. It is
available if you serve open-weight models yourself — which reframes self-hosting as
buying a monitoring surface, not only cost control. It is a concrete argument for
the [heterogeneous architecture](../5.%20AI%20Agents%20%26%20Tool%20Use/Small%20Language%20Models%20for%20Agents%20—%20The%20Heterogeneous%20Architecture.md)
where a self-hosted small model handles the tool-calling turns.

**You need labelled failures to fit the detector.** The probe is supervised. That
means the trajectory monitor does not go away: it is what produces the labels the
probe is trained on. The two layers compose in one direction only — traces label,
probes gate.

## 5. How to evaluate the claim before believing it

Reported ROC-AUC on a paper's own splits is the weakest form of evidence for a
monitor. Before wiring a 2-neuron readout into a serving path, the checks that
matter:

| Check | Why |
|---|---|
| **Distribution shift** | Fit the probe on one tool suite, test on a suite it never saw. Tool schemas are the input distribution; a probe that only works on trained schemas is a schema classifier |
| **Long-context degradation** | 6.2 measured a ~20-point recall drop from long benign context in prose monitors. Prompt-boundary probes may or may not inherit it. Measure it, do not assume |
| **Adversarial iteration** | The threshold that matters is not first-attempt detection. Assume an injected instruction is trying to look like a legitimate tool need |
| **Steering side effects** | Steering changes the residual stream for *everything downstream*, not just the call decision. Check task quality, not only call rate |
| **Model-upgrade fragility** | The basis is fit per checkpoint. Every model upgrade invalidates it. Budget re-fitting into the upgrade path or the guardrail silently stops working |

The fifth is the operational killer and the reason to keep this as a layer, never
as the only layer.

---

## 6. Where it fits in a real stack

🟢 **MVP** — do not use this. Schema validation plus tool-use examples in the tool
definition addresses validity cheaply; Anthropic reports parameter-handling accuracy
moving 72% → 90% from examples alone. Fix the interface before probing the weights.

🟡 **Production** — trajectory monitoring per 6.2, plus deterministic
postcondition checks on writes. Interface-level fixes and idempotency get you most
of the way on all three failure classes.

🔴 **Managed-grade** — a probe layer becomes worth its complexity when you (a) serve
your own weights, (b) already have labelled failure traces from the funnel, and (c)
have a latency budget that cannot absorb a judge call on every turn. That is a real
configuration — a high-QPS agent on a self-hosted small model — and it is the only
one where this pays.

> **Architectural takeaway:** the durable idea here is not PRISMS. It is that the
> monitor's *placement* is a design variable, and until now the only available
> placement was outside the model, after the fact, at the cost of an inference
> pass. Pre-generation, in-loop, near-zero-cost is a third option. It is narrow —
> supervised, per-checkpoint, white-box only — but it is a genuinely different
> point on the cost/coverage curve rather than a cheaper version of the same point.
