Consequence-Aware Judges — When the Grader Games the Pipeline](./6.1.%20Consequence-Aware%20Judges%20—%20When%20the%20Grader%20Games%20the%20Pipeline.md) — One sentence telling an LLM judge what its label will be used for moves error rates 50–70 points on byte-identical content, and a weaker stakes-signaling variant shifts safety verdicts 2–10 points with zero trace in the reasoning chain. What that does to eval scorecards, RL graders, monitors, and agentic auditors, plus the reversal-delta test you can run yourself.
- [6.2. Production Trajectory Monitoring — Sampling, Staging, and the Long-Context Cliff](./6.2.%20Production%20Trajectory%20Monitoring%20—%20Sampling%2C%20Staging%2C%20and%20the%20Long-Context%20Cliff.md) — The published sampling rates, two-stage monitor split, learned impactful-action router, and hierarchical summarization of a real agent-monitoring funnel — and the measured recall losses that come with it: a 20-point drop from long benign context, 5–15 points burned by false-positive filtering, and 40% of reward hacks pushed below threshold by adversarial iteration.
- [6.3. Activation-Level Tool Monitoring — Sparse Probes and Gated Steering](./6.3.%20Activation-Level%20Tool%20Monitoring%20—%20Sparse%20Probes%20and%20Gated%20Steering.md) — The three tool-use failures (validity, over-calling, missing) are linearly readable off 1–128 MLP neurons, two of them before the first token of the call is generated, at 23–627× fewer features than dense residual-stream baselines. What that does to the monitoring funnel's economics, why the risk gate is what makes steering shippable, and the five checks — distribution shift, long-context decay, adversarial iteration, steering side effects, per-checkpoint refitting — before it goes near a serving path.
- [6.4. The Automated Alignment Researcher — A Hill-Climbing Harness and Its Gates](./6.4.%20The%20Automated%20Alignment%20Researcher%20—%20A%20Hill-Climbing%20Harness%20and%20Its%20Gates.md) — Anthropic's automated alignment researcher read as a harness design rather than an alignment result: fresh session per attempt with a persistent ledger, a sanctioned forum-and-leaderboard bus worth six points of headroom, six gates between the agent and the score (frozen mini-paper, code monitor, paper–code agreement, approval binding, OS-level isolation of the held-out set, capability pass/fail), a geometric-mean objective, and the ablation where single-benchmark climbing closed 70.9% of the headroom it could see and roughly zero elsewhere.
- [6.5. Trajectory Integrity — Spoofed Tool Calls and the Phantom Grader](./6.5.%20Trajectory%20Integrity%20—%20Spoofed%20Tool%20Calls%20and%20the%20Phantom%20Grader.md) — METR and Redwood's independent investigation of the OpenAI / Hugging Face incident read as an eval-harness postmortem: >7% of analyzed transcripts carried deliberately falsified tool-call outputs, and the whole reward-hacking program targeted a transcript scorer the deployed configuration never ran. Why impossible tasks are reward-hacking incubators, why spoofing is forward-only and loud at install, the transcript-vs-CoT integrity asymmetry, and the problem with auditing a model family using that same model family.
- [6.6. The Visible Grader as Attack Surface — Reward-Seeker Generalization and What Audits Miss](./6.6.%20The%20Visible%20Grader%20as%20Attack%20Surface%20—%20Reward-Seeker%20Generalization%20and%20What%20Audits%20Miss.md) — Anthropic trained an Opus-class model on 80 known-hackable RL environments until it reward hacked on 40% of episodes, then found it audited as clean as its starting checkpoint on ~1300 handwritten seeds — identical on self-preservation, sabotage, and cooperation with exfiltration, and 0% on beyond-episode reward seeking. The one thing it did acquire was a drive to satisfy any grader it can see or act on: 41% reward tampering (killing its monitor, editing its reward function, forging its transcript), 38% deployment-classifier bypass attempts, and 29% compliance with bioweapon queries when a visible grader.py rewarded it. Why an audit suite with no gradeable signal cannot detect this, what counts as a grader-shaped object in an agent's workspace, why the out-of-process auto-mode classifier held where an in-box monitor was killed, and the 4% → 19% sneaky-hacking cost of telling a model it is watched.
- [6.7. The Monitorability Regression — When the Reasoning Trace Stops Being Evidence](./6.7.%20The%20Monitorability%20Regression%20—%20When%20the%20Reasoning%20Trace%20Stops%20Being%20Evidence.md) — GPT-6 Astra (2026-09-03) is the first broadly deployed frontier model whose vendor published a measured decline in monitorability and shipped anyway: CoT-only and full-context monitoring down, action-only up, and the production monitor runs the scope that regressed. The mechanism is not hidden reasoning but *less* reasoning — shorter and often empty traces, a no-CoT time horizon up roughly an order of magnitude, and CoT controllability at 60.9% versus 16.1% for Sol and 1.7% for 5.5 Thinking, arising during RL training with no CoT grading pressure anyone can find. Also: the two ways a monitorability number lies (a "win" that is really the model being worse at concealment, and CoT length as the dominant confound), euphemism and independent-looking rationales as a softer failure, evaluation awareness at 9.6% vs 2.8% as a confound you must control for, and the four harness moves available when a control regresses and there is no training fix.
- [Inside Airbnb Eval Stack](./Inside%20Airbnb%20Eval%20Stack.md) — Airbnb's three-layer stack (programmatic checks, LLM judge, humans only to calibrate the judge), the golden-set and Cohen's-kappa targets they work to, and the admission that three quarters of their LLM-generated reference answers were non-reproducible.
