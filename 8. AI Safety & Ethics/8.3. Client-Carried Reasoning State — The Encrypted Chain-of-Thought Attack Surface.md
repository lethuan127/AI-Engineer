# 8.3. Client-Carried Reasoning State — The Encrypted Chain-of-Thought Attack Surface

> Companion to [7.3. Durable Execution for Agents — Surviving Crashes Mid-Loop](../7.%20AI%20System%20Architecture/7.3.%20Durable%20Execution%20for%20Agents%20—%20Surviving%20Crashes%20Mid-Loop.md) and [MCP Goes Stateless](../5.%20AI%20Agents%20&%20Tool%20Use/MCP%20Goes%20Stateless%20—%20The%202026-07-28%20Release%20Candidate.md). Those notes argue for pushing conversation and reasoning state out of the server and back onto the client. This note is the security bill for that architecture: once hidden reasoning rides on the client, it becomes an attack surface — and in August 2026 a team turned that surface into a working data-extraction and prompt-injection attack across Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google.

Reasoning models produce a hidden chain-of-thought (CoT) the provider does not want to expose in plaintext — it leaks IP and unsummarized model internals. But in stateless, zero-retention, and client-managed tool-loop modes, *the server keeps no session*, so the reasoning has to travel to the client and back to continue a multi-turn reasoning process. The providers square this circle by returning the CoT as an **encrypted, client-held blob** (OpenAI's encrypted reasoning items, Anthropic's `signature` on thinking blocks, Google's thought signatures). The client can't read it, can't tamper with it, but must replay it verbatim on the next turn. arXiv:2608.09867 (submitted 2026-08-10) shows this design has an architectural flaw that survives the encryption being unbroken.

> Why it matters: this is the clearest case yet of a *harness* decision — where reasoning state lives — creating a *safety* problem that no amount of model alignment fixes. The blob is cryptographically intact the entire time. The vulnerability is in who can replay it and what a weaker sibling model will do with it.

---

## 1. The mechanism: portable blobs + a global key

The encrypted CoT blob is an authenticated ciphertext. You cannot flip a bit — any tampering yields an API rejection. What you *can* do is take an intact, unmodified blob from one place and replay it somewhere else. Matthew Green's May 2026 weekend investigation (the prior work the paper builds on) established the key facts empirically:

- **Replay works across sessions, across accounts, and — for OpenAI — across models.** A blob minted under one account is accepted as valid reasoning by a session running under a different account.
- **The blobs are *semantically active* on replay.** A social-security number reasoned about in session A popped out of session B, a different account, with no prompt asking for it.
- **The cross-account acceptance implies a single global encryption key**, not per-account or per-session keys. Everyone's reasoning is escrowed under one slowly-changing key.

> Architectural takeaway: "we encrypt the reasoning" is not the same claim as "the reasoning is confined to your session." Authenticated encryption stops *tampering*; it does nothing to stop *replay* unless the key or the authenticated context is scoped to the session. A global key with no session binding makes an opaque blob a portable, provider-honored capability token.

---

## 2. The scalable jailbreak: use a weaker sibling as a decoder

The paper's core contribution turns replay into extraction. You never jailbreak the strong model directly. Instead:

```text
strong model (Opus-class)          weaker sibling (Haiku-class)
   produces encrypted CoT  ──replay──▶  decrypts + transcribes
   (safeguarded, won't leak)            (less safeguarded, complies)
```

Because the blob is decryptable by any model in the same provider family (global key), you inject a strong model's encrypted trace into a weaker, less-safeguarded model from the same provider and instruct *it* to decode and print the plaintext. The strong model's safety training is never engaged. The decoders named in the disclosure: Claude Haiku 4.5 for Claude traces, GPT-5.6 Luna for GPT traces, Gemini Robotics ER-1.6 for Gemini traces.

> Lesson: a heterogeneous model family (see [Small Language Models for Agents](../5.%20AI%20Agents%20&%20Tool%20Use/Small%20Language%20Models%20for%20Agents%20—%20The%20Heterogeneous%20Architecture.md)) with a *shared* reasoning-encryption key means your weakest, cheapest, least-aligned model is a universal decoder for your strongest model's private thoughts. The security of the family is the security of its softest member.

---

## 3. Four attack vectors

The interchangeability of blobs opens four distinct abuse paths, in rising order of who-should-worry:

| Vector | What it does | Who is exposed |
|---|---|---|
| **Anti-distillation bypass** | Extract a frontier model's proprietary reasoning verbatim, defeating the whole reason CoT is hidden | The provider's IP |
| **Bulk private-data extraction** | Decode reasoning blobs from public agent logs; recover secrets the developer never saw | Anyone who published raw agent logs |
| **Hidden-hazard disclosure** | Recover harmful content the model reasoned about but *safely refused* in the visible answer | Safety guarantees that only gate visible output |
| **Invisible prompt injection** | Craft a malicious instruction inside an opaque blob, replay it into an unrelated agent run to poison it | Agentic pipelines that ingest shared traces |

The empirical scale: across 6,708 public agent trajectories the team decoded **315,320 thinking blocks**, and after excluding benchmark data recovered **367 PII artifacts and 182 credentials** — including API keys, passwords, access tokens, and private keys — from genuine user sessions. A meaningful fraction of the recovered secrets appeared *only* in the hidden reasoning and nowhere in the visible transcript, so sanitizing the readable conversation left them exposed.

---

## 4. The side channel that no key rotation fixes

Green's investigation surfaced a second, more fundamental leak that is independent of the encryption entirely. Even without reading the blob, an attacker observes its **length**, the reported `reasoning_tokens`, or just the wall-clock response time. That is enough to run a side channel:

1. Ask the model to reason about a secret bit it is forbidden to state. If the bit is 0, do cheap computation A; if 1, do expensive computation B.
2. Both produce identical *visible* output — no policy is violated.
3. But B produces far more hidden reasoning. Blob size, token count, and latency all leak the bit.

Green demonstrated extracting the byte `0xA3` bit-by-bit from timing alone. This channel leaks through chat-only interfaces where the attacker never touches the raw API.

> Architectural takeaway: encryption protects *contents*, not *the act of reasoning*. Any time a model does secret-dependent reasoning, the *volume* of that reasoning is a covert channel. You cannot patch this with better key management — the only real fix is a policy gate that runs *before* the model reasons about the sensitive value, and gating itself often requires reasoning. This is a genuinely hard, possibly unpatchable, class of leak.

---

## 5. The disclosure asymmetry — and what changed

There is an instructive governance gap in the timeline. When Green reported replay and side-channel behavior through bug-bounty channels in mid-2026, OpenAI called it unreproducible and Anthropic said it saw no security implications, offering only to update developer docs. Three months later the same behavior, turned into a scaled extraction attack with 182 recovered credentials, was hard to wave off. Per the researchers' own reproducibility statement, the main extraction attack stopped working after mitigations by August 2026 — though no provider has publicly confirmed a fix, and it is unknown whether the hundreds of thousands of *already-published* blobs remain decodable.

Current vendor guidance has shifted to push the burden onto developers: Anthropic now states thinking blocks are tied to the model that produced them and should be **stripped when switching models**. The defensive posture for anyone building on these APIs:

- **Strip encrypted reasoning items and opaque reasoning fields from any trace you publish or log.** Sanitizing visible text is not enough — secrets hide in the blob.
- **Never commit raw API transcripts**, even post-sanitization, to public repos.
- **Treat inbound chat/agent streams as untrusted for reasoning blobs.** If a malicious party can inject JSON into your conversation stream, they can inject their own reasoning blobs; sanitize inputs.
- **Assume any model in a provider family can decode the family's reasoning.** Do not rely on a strong model's refusals to protect content that a cheaper sibling in the same family will also see.

> Lesson for the harness designer: pushing state to the client (the [stateless MCP](../5.%20AI%20Agents%20&%20Tool%20Use/MCP%20Goes%20Stateless%20—%20The%202026-07-28%20Release%20Candidate.md) direction, the durable-execution direction) is the right scalability call — but client-carried *secrets* need session-scoped or account-scoped keys and authenticated context binding, not a global key. When you move sensitive state across the trust boundary, the encryption is the easy 80%; scoping who can replay it is the load-bearing 20%.

---

## 6. The vendor countermeasure — and what it costs the harness

With Fable 5.1 (2026-09-01) Anthropic shipped the first structural fix rather than a docs update, and it targets exactly the capability this note is about. **New API accounts can no longer edit Claude's prior context in a multi-turn conversation while preserving the transcript of Claude's prior thinking.** That combination — rewrite the visible turns, keep the carried reasoning blob — is what turned replay from a curiosity into an industrial extraction technique, since it lets an attacker vary the surrounding context around a fixed blob and read the differences.

The rollout is staged in a way worth noting: accounts created before the cutoff are not yet affected, but the restriction applies to all accounts on future model releases. So this is a deprecation with an undated deadline, not an opt-in.

The framing is anti-distillation, and the security benefit is a side effect — but the cost lands on legitimate harness builders:

| Harness pattern | Status |
|---|---|
| Append-only multi-turn with thinking carried forward | Unaffected. |
| Context compaction that rewrites or summarizes earlier assistant turns while preserving thinking blocks | Breaks. Compaction must now drop the reasoning blobs it summarizes over. |
| Trajectory replay / eval fixtures that reconstruct a mid-conversation state including prior thinking | Breaks for new accounts. Snapshot-style evals must construct state *without* carried reasoning, or accept its loss. |
| Model routing that hands a partially-reasoned conversation to a sibling model | Already discouraged (blocks are model-bound); now increasingly unavailable. |

> Architectural takeaway: **client-carried reasoning state is being walked back toward append-only.** A harness that treats the messages array as freely editable — the assumption behind most compaction and replay tooling — is building on a capability the providers are actively removing, for reasons that have nothing to do with your use case. Design compaction to discard reasoning blobs rather than carry them, and keep eval fixtures reconstructible from visible turns alone.

### 6.1 The countermeasure has a name, and the attack has operator telemetry

Anthropic's 2026-09-10 misuse report names the restriction **preserved thinking** and states its motive plainly: it "stops new API accounts from altering the system prompt, tools, or messages that precede Claude's reasoning in multi-turn conversations… because editing the context before it is a common technique attackers use to make Claude reveal it."

The same report upgrades the attack in §2–§4 from a research result to confirmed practice at industrial scale. The provider returns "a reference to its raw thinking as a 'thinking signature' instead of the raw thinking"; one named lab "was able to circumvent this control… by saving the reasoning signature from Claude's response, starting a new session, and eliciting Claude to convert the reasoning signature back into the full reasoning trace." Cross-session replay of an opaque handle — the precise failure this note predicts from a non-session-scoped key — was run as a distillation pipeline, one campaign exceeding 151M exchanges in three months.

Two further countermeasures are named, both of which change what a harness can read back:

| Control | Effect on the harness |
|---|---|
| **Reasoning summarization** — "Claude now summarizes its internal reasoning before responding" | The trace you log is a summary, not the trace the model used. Debugging and monitorability tooling that assumed fidelity is now reading a lossy artifact ([6.7](../6.%20Evaluation%20&%20Observability/6.7.%20The%20Monitorability%20Regression%20—%20When%20the%20Reasoning%20Trace%20Stops%20Being%20Evidence.md)). |
| **Adversarial-extraction classifiers**, strengthened at the Fable 5 launch | Request-level, and therefore subject to the decomposition bypass documented in [8.11 §4](./8.11.%20The%20Adversary's%20Harness%20—%20What%20Misuse%20Telemetry%20Says%20About%20Your%20Own%20Stack.md). |

> Lesson: the mitigation arrived as an anti-distillation measure, not as a security fix, and it lands on legitimate harnesses either way. Read the provider's *commercial* threat model, not just its security advisories — it is the better predictor of which API capability disappears next.
